# Explicit complete loss and normal deletion

An owner-approved complete deletion could not progress after an engine died
with an unproved final save. Ordinary Delete correctly retained the unsaved
source, and raw Stop supersession did not constitute loss authority.

Require an immutable, exact owner-loss receipt and one normal Delete in the
same transaction. Retain original capture/failure facts and give the Delete
separate dispatch authority, never final-save proof. Keep compute settlement,
provider deletion and all physical erasure checks on the existing workers.

A new source guard also initially blocked legitimate boot/writer retirement
after positive provider deletion. Test the whole worker flow with a previously
ready, delivered, unsealed writer as well as a never-ready engine. An empty
boot fixture cannot exercise retirement. Retain missing seals as missing while
permitting exact terminal retirement only after its provider evidence.

Independent review found that the exact Delete could be revived after a real
terminal worker outcome, and that a lifecycle UPDATE could change the approved
source while the first receipt was still uncommitted. Require terminal Delete
rows to remain unchanged, and acquire the parent workspace lock before testing
whether a receipt exists on both INSERT and UPDATE. Retain the real application
role race tests and their original assertions; a later read of the receipt is
not serialization with its admission.

The recovery row can also fill a previously null custody ID. Serialize those
fact changes before receipt admission, excluding only the same retry clocks
excluded from the approval. Validate existing recovery facts against the
approved history before recomputing its disposition digest. Retain the
independent application-role custody race and its pre/post-admission controls.

Check legal holds again after preparatory provider I/O, including blobs reached
through workspace references. An admission-only check does not protect a hold
that arrives while the provider call is pending. Keep actual object references
in erasure fixtures so a failed object delete cannot pass without doing I/O.

Immutable receipts must survive workspace data purge without blocking final
organization erasure. Permit their owning-workspace cascade only after the
parent is gone, including dependent dispositions; keep direct deletion refused
while that parent exists. Exercise the actual organization deletion worker
after provider journals, physical objects and Computer templates are retired.
An older accounting RESTRICT barrier still blocks complete organization
erasure. Retain the unchanged failure and its parent discriminator separately.
An explicitly scoped diagnostic may remove its one synthetic settled lease as
the fixture owner to prove the new cascades. That diagnostic is not a product
accounting cleanup or a complete organization-erasure pass. Keep accounting
retention unchanged and record its barrier as a separate follow-up.

These contracts are covered by the owner-discard-delete real-PG suites,
restricted-login CLI, migration exception mutation checks and the exact
authority/atomic-receipt eval seeds. The original failed assertion is retained
privately; the shared normal-Delete refusal remains a permanent control.
