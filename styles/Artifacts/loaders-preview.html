The app's loaders, rendered as standalone HTML from mirrors of the production components in apps/desktop/src/renderer/shared/ui/loading/. The active turn's rail and running tool rows show the glass square: the same square in soft glass colour combos, running like Stream in one of three motions picked at random (the last section).

Agent — Puzzle

apps/desktop/src/renderer/shared/ui/loading/zeros-spinner.tsx (variant="agent") · puzzle-motion.ts · a 4 × 4 sliding-tile square · four corner anchors hold the square while three hands slide tiles through the channel at once · --loader-active

A self-solving puzzle worked by three hands at once, each on its own loose clock (a move every 300–460ms), so two or three tiles are usually gliding. A hand pulls the neighbouring tile into its hole, or now and then pushes two in a line like a real 15-puzzle, in short runs that never step straight back. Cells under a move in flight are reserved until it lands, so tiles never cross. Six holes keep the square airy; the corners never move, so it never loses its outline. Tiles sit on whole device pixels: 2.5px at 12–14px, 3px at 16px, 4.5px at 24px.

Where it shows

Chat tabs (in each agent's colours) and sidebar workspace rows. Every mount starts from its own random square and runs its own random hands, so no two show the same shape or march in step; hidden retained surfaces pause their hands until they are visible again. The transcript (the active turn's rail and running tool and subagent rows) shows the glass square instead: the last section.

Same tiles and square as the workspace glyphs, opposite motion. The puzzle shuffles at random; Run only ever moves forward: every 95ms the next row, top to bottom, slides one tile right in 140ms and wraps round at the edge, so the whole square streams left to right, one full pass every 1.5s. Rows are drawn as single shapes, so tiles side by side never show a seam.

Colour carries the PR, motion carries the agent, and the square stays the workspace's mark, all in one 16px slot: a working agent no longer hides the PR state.

At rest the glyph is the workspace's own square in --fg3 at 25%. While its agent works the same square runs the Puzzle in --fg2; when the turn ends it stops where it is and resumes from there on the next run, so a workspace comes back from each run in a new shape. It keeps that shape while its row briefly shows something else.

Colour check: closed and conflicts share one red, and in Dark --red-fg sits right next to the open brown (new-york and lisbon vs palembang). The toggle under PR status × agent state compares --red-primary.

A plan to review or a question to answer never replaces the square: the mark sits in the row's inset gutter, left of it (flat rows use the pencil's slot, as chat tabs do). While the turn waits on the answer the square rests; beside a question the agent keeps working through (Codex's optional asks), it keeps moving.

Unchanged: the design pen, plain folders (the agent Puzzle while they work), archiving (Orbit), and Ungrouped rows, which lead with the repository icon and keep a trailing agent loader.

Gibberish agent thinking — the phrases

          

          
Settings → Experimental → Gibberish agent thinking (off by default) puts these on the turn rail, between the square and the timer, at 13px, a new one every 3 seconds, each shimmering once as it shows (apps/desktop/src/renderer/shared/ui/loading/gibberish-thinking.ts). A hundred agent-at-work phrases of up to three words, misspelled so they still read, and sound, like themselves: every word keeps its first consonant and about its length while the vowels wander. A turn goes through all hundred in its own order, shuffled from its start time, before any comes round again. Screen readers skip it.

        

        

          
The combos

          

          
Four to six colours per combo, each kept to a breath of its hue. Each swatch is a combo's field running diagonally; a live combo runs it along either diagonal, left or right, at random, and the square shows it through its tiles, sheen and glow included.
