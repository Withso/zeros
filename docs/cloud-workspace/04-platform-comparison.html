04 PLATFORM COMPARISON · JULY 2026

Daytona + Railway vs Vercel + Fly.io — the actual call

It's two decisions, not one: who rents us the computers the agents run on, and who runs the front desk (login, records, pings). Here's the verdict on both — with the receipts. Updated 2026-07-02: the front desk now also keeps the cloud record — the always-on database that permanently stores your chats and history. Same winners; stronger case.

The short version

Two verdicts, sixty seconds

Conductor, our reference competitor, made these two choices separately too: Vercel for the computers, Fly.io for the front desk. We should keep them separate as well — and here's where each lands.

🗓️ Decision update — 2026-07-02

The founder decided to adopt the cloud record — our cloud database that permanently stores your chats, sessions, and workspace history in your account. For this page that changes the weight of Decision B, not its winner: the backend is no longer just a login desk plus a small registry; it now hosts an always-on Postgres (the record) and a sync engine as first-class requirements. Decision A (Daytona) is untouched. Sections below carry dated notes where the analysis moved.

✅ Decision A — the computers: stay on Daytona

Run the Phase 1 spike, keep the escape hatch warm. Daytona is the only contender with a documented WebSocket-over-preview-URL path (our direct Mac-to-sandbox connection), has two regions (US + EU vs Vercel's US-only), the cheapest per-vCPU rate, and $200 free credit with a path to $10k–$50k startup credits. Its risks — closed-source move in June 2026, experimental fork/snapshot — don't touch anything v1 depends on.

✅ Decision B — the front desk: Supabase, not Railway — now stronger

Supabase for auth + the cloud record + realtime — the same pick as before, and the record makes the case stronger, because Supabase's managed Postgres becomes the record itself. A tiny worker on Cloudflare or Railway for provisioning, R2/Railway Buckets for backup blobs, and ElectricSQL as the named sync-engine spike (Conductor-proven; runs on any Postgres, so it doesn't lock the database host). Railway's honest best roles: the thin worker now, or plain-Postgres record host later — not replacing Supabase today.

Decision B

Who runs the front desk?

This section originally argued the front desk could stay deliberately small, because the heavy stuff (chat, files, the agent) lived only with the engine in the sandbox. Updated 2026-07-02: the live experience still runs in the sandbox, but the permanent copy of chats and history now lives here too. Six jobs, two of them new or upgraded.

🗓️ Decision update — 2026-07-02

The founder decided that all durable workspace data — chats, agent sessions, workspace metadata, memberships — lives in the cloud record: an always-on Postgres in the user's account, synced live to every signed-in device (part 05 covers the how). Decision B therefore now includes hosting the record and its sync engine as first-class requirements — this layer is no longer just a login desk with a small ledger. The verdict below was re-checked against the heavier job: it doesn't change, it gets stronger.

1

Auth

Users sign in; the Mac app and the sandbox both need to verify "this is really Arun."

2

The cloud record upgraded

An always-on Postgres keeping the workspace registry and permanently storing every chat, session, and piece of history in your account. The sandbox is the workbench; this is the filing cabinet — workbenches get cleared, the filing cabinet doesn't.

3

Sync engine new

Streams every chat/session event from a running engine into the record in near-real-time, and syncs it back out to every signed-in device. Named spike: ElectricSQL — Conductor-proven, runs on any Postgres. Supabase Realtime is the simpler starter.

4

Realtime fan-out

A lightweight "something changed, refresh" ping to your other devices. The live chat stream still does not go through here — it stays on the direct Mac↔sandbox WebSocket (the live wire); the record gets its copy via the engine's write-through.

5

Object storage

Cheap durable blobs — now demoted to a backup safety net (periodic exports of the record), not the primary home of chat history.

6

Provisioning worker

A tiny server-side program that holds the Daytona API key and creates sandboxes on request — so the secret key never ships inside the Mac app.

The gentle-but-critical nuance

Here's the thing to hold onto when comparing these three:

Supabase

Serviced office

$25/month gets you:

  • The receptionist — auth, 100k monthly users included
  • The filing system — managed Postgres with row-level security (database-enforced rules like "users can only read their own rows")
  • The intercom — Realtime, 500 concurrent connections included
  • 100 GB of storage

One vendor, four of the six needs met — and since 2026-07-02 its Postgres is load-bearing: it becomes the cloud record, the permanent home of every user's chats and history, with ElectricSQL able to run on top of it. Verified pricing

Railway

Excellent empty office space
  • Best-in-class developer experience; cheap usage-based compute (a small always-on API ≈ $5/month)
  • Genuinely great object storage — Buckets: $0.015/GB-month with free egress
  • ✗ But no auth, no realtime service, no row-level security — Railway's own guides point you at Clerk or self-hosted Supabase for login
  • ✗ Its Postgres is officially an unmanaged template ("you have total control over their configuration and maintenance"); high-availability shipped March 2026 explicitly experimental; point-in-time recovery is weeks old
  • ~ New nuance since 2026-07-02: ElectricSQL runs on any Postgres, so Railway is a technically viable host for the cloud record — a legitimate scale-out or preference option later. But handing the permanent copy of every user's chats to an unmanaged template with this reliability history is a deliberate future choice, not the default

Verified

Fly.io

Same shape as Railway
  • Compute + a database, nothing above it
  • More regions — 18, including Mumbai and Singapore
  • Cheaper machines (~$2/month)
  • A genuinely managed Postgres (HA, backups, pooling on all plans) starting at $38/month
  • ✗ You still build auth and realtime yourself

Verified

Plain-words glossary — every term used on this page
Sandbox — a rented computer in a data center that can be created and thrown away in seconds; the "hotel room" the agent works in.
Auth — the login system; proves "this is really you" to our app and servers.
The cloud record — our cloud database (an always-on Postgres) that permanently stores your chats, sessions, and workspace history in your account; the filing cabinet (added 2026-07-02).
Registry DB — the old name for the small guest-ledger part of the database (which workspaces exist, who may enter); upgraded into the cloud record on 2026-07-02.
Sync engine — the layer that streams engine events into the cloud record and syncs it out to your devices; ElectricSQL (Conductor-proven, runs on any Postgres) is our named spike candidate.
The live wire — the direct Mac↔sandbox WebSocket carrying live chat and files; no broker in the middle, unchanged by the record.
Realtime — a "push notification" channel between our servers and your apps: "something changed, refresh."
Object storage — cheap cloud space for files/blobs; since 2026-07-02 a backup safety net behind the cloud record, not chat's primary home.
Postgres — the industry-standard relational database; the filing system behind most web apps.
Row-level security (RLS) — database-enforced rules like "users can only read their own rows."
MicroVM — a tiny virtual machine with its own kernel; a stronger wall between tenants than a shared-kernel container.
WebSocket (WSS) — a persistent two-way connection; how the Mac talks to the sandbox directly, no middleman.
Egress allowlist — a list of outside addresses a sandbox is allowed to call; anything else is blocked.
SLA — a contractual uptime promise, with penalties if the provider misses it.
BYOC — "bring your own cloud": running the vendor's software on computers we rent directly.
CloudSandboxProvider — our one thin code layer that speaks "create/start/stop/connect a sandbox," so the vendor behind it can be swapped; a universal power adapter.
Mutagen file mirror — the piece of our design that keeps a local copy of files in sync, hiding network distance.
COGS — cost of goods sold; here, what we pay providers per user we serve.
MAU — monthly active users.
